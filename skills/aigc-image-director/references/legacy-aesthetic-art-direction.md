## Core principle

Treat the image as a directed visual statement, not a container for every attractive element. A strong frame has an authored visual thesis, one clear emotional sentence, one dominant focal subject or relationship, a deliberate shape rhythm, a meaningful tension, an economical palette, motivated light, and enough quiet space for important forms to breathe. Cleanliness protects this direction; it does not replace it.

For cinematic work, require four linked decisions: the composition has a motive, the eye has a designed path, the colors have a theme, and the frame holds a causally readable narrative state. If these four are absent, polish only makes the image more expensive-looking, not more cinematic.

Do not repair a weak image by adding more adjectives, detail, fog, particles, glow, or saturation. First decide what the viewer must notice in the first three seconds, what makes the frame distinctive, and what visual relationship carries the feeling. Then remove conflicts without flattening the chosen character.

## Research-informed cinematic grammar

- Treat lighting and composition as one decision. A light direction that does not reinforce the subject's placement, gesture, or destination will feel pasted on even when the color grade is attractive.
- Use lower-key lighting for shape, tension, and selective attention—not as a universal dark filter. A wrapped key, soft backlight, negative fill, or controlled practical source must reveal why the frame is dark and what remains important.
- Motivate every camera choice by the emotional beat. A low angle can express threat or power; a wide lens can express immersion or scale; a compressed view can express distance or surveillance. Do not select a dramatic angle merely because it sounds cinematic.
- Let color relationships carry identity. Prefer a dominant hue, a supporting hue, and a neutral or contaminated transition between them over isolated, fully saturated color blocks. Warm/cool interaction should be caused by sources or materials, not pasted on as a teal-orange filter.
- Make lines end somewhere meaningful. Leading lines, shadows, rails, beams, sword directions, gazes, and paths should converge on the focal subject or the intended destination; if they leave the frame, that exit must be the story.

Public creator examples and social posts are most useful as visual studies, not as magic prompt recipes. Read them in this order: large shapes and crop → light source and value grouping → color interaction → medium and surface behavior. Ignore unverified “quality keyword” piles, exact creator signatures, and model-specific claims that do not apply to the target tool.

Research-informed refinement: cinematic frames need readable large-scale organization, but readability does not mean visual poverty. Keep two or three story colors, let the background support rather than compete, and create richness through shape rhythm, scale variation, counterpoint, material contrast, and selective detail. Texture/grain must earn its place. A wide frame should increase spatial meaning and negative space, not the number of unassigned objects.

## Medium and realism routing

Do not treat photorealism as the neutral setting. Select a medium and a realism scope independently:

| User need | Medium direction | Keep realistic | May remain stylized |
|---|---|---|---|
| real-person portrait or likeness | live-action, editorial, painterly portrait, graphic portrait, or chosen hybrid | identity, face structure, anatomy, hands, wardrobe logic | background, palette, brush/line/shape treatment, atmosphere |
| fantasy character or wuxia hero | concept painting, ink, 2D, stylized 3D, graphic key art, or live-action if requested | silhouette, gesture, weapon contact, spatial logic | face rendering, texture language, palette, environment treatment |
| environment or architecture | matte painting, concept art, ink wash, watercolor, graphic design, stylized 3D, or photography | perspective, scale, structural anchors, light direction when requested | surface rendering, clouds, material simplification, atmospheric abstraction |
| abstract, poster, collection, or visual metaphor | graphic, collage, print, painterly, 3D, or another explicit medium | only the shapes, text, objects, or relationships the user locks | almost all surface treatment and atmosphere |

If the user says only “氛围感”, do not silently add realistic skin, pores, lens bokeh, photographic grain, or physically rendered material microtexture. Choose the medium from the subject and the intended emotional effect, then use camera language only when it helps that medium.

For reference-led work, separate `IDENTITY / CONTENT` from `MEDIUM / SURFACE`. A real face reference can control identity while a watercolor, ink, painterly, 2D, or stylized 3D treatment controls the final surface. Do not let a reference image's photographic finish overwrite the user's requested medium.

## Art-direction sequence

0. **Aesthetic thesis and tension:** state what the frame is really about and the two forces that give it energy, such as order versus disruption or stillness versus imminent motion. Add one signature gesture or controlled surprise.

1. **Emotional and narrative beat:** state the visual moment in one sentence: arrival, waiting, threat, revelation, aftermath, pursuit, or another concrete beat. Note what just happened and what can follow.
2. **Composition motive:** explain why the frame uses its main geometry, crop, or negative space. Remove any arrangement that has no story or viewing function.
   Allow one purposeful counter-structure when it performs a different job, such as ordered architecture versus an off-axis human gesture or a straight path versus a curved fabric gesture.
3. **Focal anchor:** choose one primary subject or relationship. Make it win through at least two of position, scale, value, color, edge clarity, focus, or gesture.
4. **Eye path:** map entry → focal anchor → secondary discovery → rest/exit. Let lines, gazes, light, value, overlap, and focus reinforce that path.
5. **Shape rhythm:** choose one dominant geometry—leading lines, frame-within-frame, diagonal action, symmetry, or deliberate negative space. Repeated forms should support this rhythm, not compete with it.
6. **Color economy and theme:** choose a dominant color, a supporting color, and a neutral field. State what the palette means in this scene. Use the strongest saturation only where it helps the focal or narrative pressure point.
7. **Value design:** check the image in grayscale. The subject, background, and important prop must separate before color grading. Preserve white detail in bright scenes and midtone structure in dark scenes.
8. **Light motivation:** define where the key light comes from and what it reveals. Use fill or negative fill to shape the subject; never use darkness or bloom as a substitute for direction.
9. **Material hierarchy:** give the focal material the clearest texture and edge response. Let supporting objects read through larger forms and a few material cues. Do not give every surface equal micro-detail.
10. **Atmospheric restraint:** choose one main air effect, such as cloud, mist, rain, dust, smoke, or bloom. Define its source, direction, depth, and density. Keep it out of the focal silhouette unless the obstruction is the story.
11. **Medium lock:** decide whether the result should read as a live-action still, photographic concept frame, stylized illustration, or another medium. Keep camera, light, texture, and rendering behavior consistent with that choice.

Use a staged construction mindset: first make the base composition readable, then tune light and color, then decide whether a single atmosphere or surface-texture pass is needed. This is a planning order, not permission to launch repeated generations without the user's authorization.

## Composition choice matrix

Choose one primary structure and one emotional purpose; do not stack every composition label.

| Emotional or narrative purpose | Preferred structure | Protect | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| journey, arrival, revelation | asymmetrical leading lines toward a destination or figure | a clear vanishing point and a readable destination | lines exit the frame, destination is overcrowded, figure becomes a speck |
| ceremony, order, authority | centered symmetry or a strong axial view | true verticals, rhythm, and a deliberate central anchor | dead symmetry with no emotional beat, repeated details at equal strength |
| loneliness, scale, transcendence | negative space with a small but readable scale reference | breathing room, horizon/value separation, and one meaningful empty field | empty spectacle, clipped sky, or a subject that disappears |
| threat, duel, visual impact | diagonal gesture, low or ground-level angle, controlled foreground obstruction | silhouette, contact points, action direction, and enough lead room | generic hero pose, too many diagonals, or a cropped weapon/hand |
| mystery, observation, reveal | frame-within-frame or partial occlusion | the subject's key contour and a motivated reveal path | decorative obstruction that hides the story |
| intimacy, character emotion | closer crop, eye-level or slightly off-axis view, restrained depth | face, gaze, hands, and a quiet background value | excessive blur, centered passport framing, or lost gesture |

## Complexity budget

Default to one primary subject, up to two supporting anchors, and one dominant atmosphere medium as a starting hierarchy. Allow more when the narrative or chosen visual language needs them, and assign every added element a role, a depth layer, and a reason to exist.

Use a detail gradient:

`focal subject > supporting anchors > architecture/environment > atmosphere`

As objects recede, reduce their size, contrast, saturation, edge clarity, and texture density. Repetition creates rhythm only when the repeated elements become subordinate; repeated high-contrast ornaments create visual noise.

## Two recurring failure patterns

### Bright red-and-white architectural spectacle

Use white cloud or pale sky as controlled negative space, not as a clipped white void. Separate the red-clad subject from red architecture through placement, value, silhouette, or a small complementary rim light. Let the architecture establish scale with a few strong columns, beams, or vanishing lines that terminate at the subject or the cloud destination; do not make every lantern, petal, carving, reflection, and distant building equally vivid. Keep one clear destination in the cloud field, preserve cloud volume, and use only sparse, directional atmospheric motion. If the red costume shares the red architecture, create separation with a quieter neutral band, a change in value, or a clean silhouette—not more red saturation.

### Low-key black-clad swordsman

Choose a precise time-slice—waiting before the strike, the instant of commitment, or the aftermath—so the pose has narrative tension. Use the sword as a secondary directional accent, not a replacement for the character's face and silhouette. Separate black clothing from the background with a motivated cool rim, controlled fill, or a value shift in the environment. Preserve readable folds, hands, face planes, and blade contact. Keep the background quiet, leave lead room in the sword/action direction, and keep the rain or debris sparse and directional. A dark mood requires shaped shadow, not a crushed-black frame.

## Aesthetic review gates

Before delivery, run these quick tests:

- **Three-second test:** at a small preview size, is the intended subject and emotional beat obvious immediately?
- **Thesis test:** can the frame be summarized as one visual sentence with a clear tension, rather than a list of attractive objects?
- **Signature test:** is there one restrained, story-relevant choice that makes the image memorable and authored?
- **Variety test:** does the frame contain intentional variation in scale, interval, edge, or shape without becoming noisy or procedural?
- **Grayscale test:** do the focal subject and important prop separate from the background without relying on hue?
- **Squint test:** when fine detail disappears, does the large-form composition still look intentional?
- **Accent test:** are the strongest saturated and brightest accents serving the subject rather than decorations?
- **Effect-removal test:** if fog, particles, bloom, or grain were removed, would the image still have a coherent composition and light design?
- **Material test:** are texture and sharpness concentrated where the viewer needs information, rather than spread uniformly across the frame?
- **Line-end test:** do the strongest lines, gazes, shadows, and gestures terminate at the intended subject or destination rather than pulling the eye out of frame?
- **Light-story test:** can the viewer infer the source and narrative purpose of the key light, fill, rim, or shadow without relying on a generic color grade?
- **Mode test:** can the image be named by one coherent visual mode or medium, rather than a stack of incompatible references?
- **Motive test:** if the main line, crop, foreground shape, or negative space were removed, can you state what narrative or viewing function is lost?
- **Eye-flow test:** can you name the first read, second read, and final rest/exit without guessing?
- **Color-theme test:** can you state what the dominant color relationship means, and is the strongest saturation serving that meaning?
- **Continuity test:** does the frame imply a before/after through pose, gaze, prop state, environmental cause, or continuation space?

If a gate fails, repair the highest-level cause in this order: focal hierarchy → shape/composition → value and light → color economy → material scale → atmosphere → small texture.

## Translation rules

- “电影感” means a coherent shot size, camera position, perspective, focus behavior, motivated light, value structure, palette, and spatial depth.
- “氛围感” means an emotional relationship between light, color, space, and limited atmosphere; it does not mean automatic fog, grain, glow, or floating particles.
- “高级感” means restraint, hierarchy, material credibility, controlled edges, and intentional negative space; it does not mean more detail or more contrast.
- “视觉冲击” means a clear scale, angle, silhouette, diagonal, gesture, or value break; it does not mean maximal clutter, saturation, or darkness.
