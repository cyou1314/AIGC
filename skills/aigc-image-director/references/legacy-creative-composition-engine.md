# Creative composition engine

Use this reference when the user asks for a beautiful, premium, cinematic, striking, memorable, atmospheric, or less-generic image, or when a previous result is technically clean but aesthetically flat.

## Core principle

A clean image is not automatically a beautiful image. Start with an authored point of view: what the frame means, what visual tension it holds, and what makes it recognizably this image rather than a generic version of the subject. Treat cleanliness as the final release gate, not as the creative idea.

For a new image, form a compact internal art direction brief:

- `AESTHETIC THESIS`: one sentence about the feeling or visual idea the frame is expressing.
- `EMOTIONAL TENSION`: two forces that give the image energy, such as order versus disruption, intimacy versus distance, stillness versus imminent motion, or beauty versus danger.
- `PRIMARY SHAPE RHYTHM`: the dominant geometry or silhouette family: arches, verticals, diagonals, S-curves, nested frames, circles, broken planes, or organic masses.
- `SIGNATURE GESTURE`: one memorable pose, crop, light break, material contrast, scale relationship, or line endpoint.
- `CONTROLLED SURPRISE`: one unusual but story-relevant choice; never a pile of random novelty.
- `SURFACE VOICE`: the medium-specific way edges, marks, materials, and color transitions behave.

Do not force every slot into the final prompt. Keep the strongest four to six decisions and make them visible through positive language.

## Creative-first sequence

1. State the emotional moment and visual premise before choosing a camera or style label.
2. Choose a composition family from the matrix below based on the story function, not on habit.
3. Choose one visual tension and one signature gesture. If the result could describe any image, the direction is under-authored.
4. Stage foreground, midground, and background as different jobs: frame or scale, action or subject, then place or emotional field.
5. Choreograph light to reveal the focal relationship and to create a value pattern that survives grayscale.
6. Build a color melody: dominant field, supporting note, neutral transition, and one pressure-point accent.
7. Choose the medium and its surface voice. A painterly image needs designed masses and mark rhythm; a photo needs lens, light, and material behavior; a graphic image needs shape and crop authority.
8. Run the clean-image gate after the beauty pass. Remove artifacts and accidental competition without removing the chosen tension, texture, asymmetry, or signature gesture.

For vague briefs, internally sketch two or three genuinely different art-direction hypotheses, such as quiet lyrical, forceful graphic, and immersive environmental. Select the strongest one; do not blend all hypotheses into a compromise prompt.

## Composition family matrix

| Family | Use when | Visual engine | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Path / destination | arrival, journey, revelation, pursuit | leading lines, one-point depth, S-curve, or a path that ends at a meaningful destination | lines become decoration or lead out of frame |
| Impact / collision | threat, duel, action, visual shock | one dominant diagonal, low or ground-level viewpoint, contact and lead room | multiple diagonals and a poster-like pose |
| Scale / sublime | isolation, transcendence, worldbuilding | small readable figure against a shaped mass or expansive field | subject becomes an unreadable speck |
| Gaze / intimacy | emotion, decision, portrait, relationship | close crop, off-axis placement, eye or hand gesture, breathing room | passport framing or empty blur |
| Ceremony / order | authority, ritual, architecture, collection | centered axis, repetition, measured spacing, controlled symmetry | dead symmetry and equal-detail ornament |
| Reveal / mystery | concealment, observation, discovery | frame-within-frame, occlusion, reflection, partial information | obstruction hides the focal evidence |
| Relationship / group | dialogue, conflict, group reaction | triangle, gaze chain, depth staging, unequal attention | every person competes equally |
| Graphic / symbolic | poster, metaphor, key art, stylized image | flat shape hierarchy, bold crop, limited palette, designed negative space | vague symbolism and decorative clutter |
| Material / still life | product, object, food, costume, surface study | contour, contact, reflection, rhythm, and controlled scale changes | equal emphasis and texture everywhere |

Choose one primary family. Add one counter-structure only when it performs a different job, for example: symmetrical architecture plus an off-axis human gesture; a straight corridor plus a soft S-curve of fabric; a quiet wide field plus one sharp action diagonal. A rule may be broken when the break is the story.

## Shape, rhythm, and visual counterweight

- Give the frame an anchor and a counterweight. The counterweight may be a distant building, a light opening, a color block, an empty field, or a secondary figure; it must answer the focal subject rather than compete with it.
- Use repetition with variation: change size, interval, occlusion, angle, or value as forms recede. Perfect repetition feels procedural; uncontrolled variation feels dirty.
- Let one line, silhouette, gaze, shadow, or color accent carry the eye. A second device may create counterpoint; every additional strong device needs a distinct narrative job.
- Use negative space as active material. Clouds, darkness, water, wall, fog, or softened architecture can carry scale, waiting, direction, or silence; empty space is not leftover space.
- In 21:9, use width to create a relationship, reveal, opposition, or scale contrast. Do not turn the wide frame into a catalogue of objects.
- For close portraits, character sheets, product shots, and graphic layouts, protect the deliverable's natural reading. An explicit ratio or layout instruction overrides the default 21:9 rule.

## Beauty pass before clean pass

Check the image in this order:

1. `THESIS`: can the frame be described as one visual sentence rather than a list of objects?
2. `SILHOUETTE`: is the primary shape memorable and readable at thumbnail size?
3. `COMPOSITION`: do scale, crop, counterweight, and eye path create a reason to look around the frame?
4. `VALUE`: does the grayscale pattern establish depth and hierarchy before color?
5. `COLOR`: does the palette have a dominant mood and a meaningful accent rather than many loud colors?
6. `SURFACE`: does the medium have a specific edge, mark, material, or transition language?
7. `SIGNATURE`: is there one restrained choice that makes the image feel authored?
8. `CLEAN`: remove random noise, muddy collisions, broken edges, and accidental competition without sterilizing the designed surface.

## Reference and online-study method

When studying a strong image, do not copy the subject or creator signature. Record: crop and scale; dominant shapes and line endpoints; foreground/midground/background jobs; value grouping; palette and color transition; light source and focal reveal; material or mark behavior; and the one memorable decision. Translate those observations into roles in the user's image.

Useful research anchors:

- [Pixar in a Box: Visual language](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/visual-language/v/storytelling-line) — line, shape, space, movement, tone, and color carry meaning.
- [Pixar in a Box: Color scripts](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/art-of-lighting/introduction-to-virtual-lighting/v/colorscripts?t=406) — color and value can map an emotional arc before final lighting.
- [Adobe: Photography composition](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/technique/composition.html?mv2=sf_internallink) — balance, leading lines, perspective, focus, and visual weight work together.
- [ARRI: Valentina Caniglia on cinematography](https://www.arri.com/news-en/the-cinematography-of-valentina-caniglia-asc-ccs) — start from story, treat scenes like paintings, and adapt lenses and light to the emotion.
- [Creative Bloq / ImagineFX: Matte-painting structure](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/digital-art/how-to-structure-a-matte-painting-to-create-a-cinematic-environment-game-concept-or-personal-illustration) — thumbnail layout, perspective, value block-in, light focus, limited color, atmosphere, and selective detail are staged decisions.
- [80 Level: 2D and 3D in concept art](https://80.lv/articles/using-2d-and-3d-in-concept-art) and [Sarah Zhong: Color Script](https://www.sarahzhong.com/color-script) — study shape, color variation, and visual development as design processes rather than finished-image effects.
- [CG Boost: Cinematic storytelling examples](https://www.cgboost.com/courses/master-cinematic-storytelling-in-blender) and [Behance: Key Frame Cinematic Illustration](https://www.behance.net/gallery/107859025/Key-Frame-Cinematic-Illustration) — compare silhouette, scale, color temperature, and narrative framing across different visual surfaces.

Use these sources for transferable principles and visual analysis, not for copying exact prompts or a living creator's signature.
