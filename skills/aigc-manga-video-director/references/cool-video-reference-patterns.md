# Cool Video Reference Patterns

Use this reference when the user wants a shot, storyboard, or Seedance prompt to feel `帅`, `酷`, `燃`, `炫`, `炸`, `有压迫感`, `有气场`, `动作高级`, `像高燃剪辑`, `像动漫打斗`, or `像电影动作场面`.

This file summarizes reusable directing grammar learned from public action-scene analysis, action comedy analysis, fight storyboard guidance, anime motion discussion, smear-frame animation, and dynamic camera-rotation references. Do not copy any specific copyrighted shot. Extract the pattern, then adapt it to the user's original character, scene, and project.

## How To Use This Reference

Do not write `帅气`, `酷炫`, or `燃` as the main instruction. Pick one pattern below and translate it into:

- shot size and lens feeling
- camera path and endpoint
- body posture and action beat
- foreground/background depth
- environment reaction
- sound/rhythm
- final held image

Use this hierarchy:

1. **Readable setup**: audience understands where the character is and who has power.
2. **Preparation beat**: pause, breath, eye movement, grip, weight shift.
3. **Release**: one clear action or camera move.
4. **Aftereffect**: cloth, dust, sparks, rain, opponent reaction, echo, silence.
5. **Endpoint**: hold the frame long enough to feel the character's status.

## Wide Choreography Spectacle

Use when the action itself is impressive and should be seen clearly: sword fight, gun-fu, martial arts, group fight, chase, dance-like battle, or one-vs-many.

Visual grammar:

- Use medium-wide or wide shot so the full body, weapon path, and opponent spacing remain readable.
- Prefer smooth tracking, dolly, Steadicam-like follow, or controlled lateral movement.
- Use slower cutting or one clear shot segment when the choreography is good.
- Keep deeper focus if multiple bodies and weapons must remain readable.
- Use colored/hard lighting or strong shape separation to make the figure pop against the background.

Prompt controls:

- `full-body readable choreography`
- `camera tracks laterally at waist height`
- `deep focus keeps opponent spacing clear`
- `one clean dodge and counterstrike, no chaotic cuts`
- `end on a held stance with opponents separated in depth`

When to avoid:

- If choreography is weak or only one still frame exists, do not fake complexity. Use a simple pose/action with strong silhouette instead.

## Close Impact Compression

Use when a hit, grab, blade near-face, punch, or threat must feel powerful even in a tight frame.

Visual grammar:

- Use over-shoulder or near-foreground framing to compress distance.
- Let foreground weapon/arm cross close to camera.
- Use longer-lens feeling or shallow depth to make distance feel dangerous.
- Keep the action direction simple: toward camera, across foreground, or into frame edge.
- Add a reaction beat in the same or next frame: head snap, eye blink, cloth shake, sparks, breath stop.

Prompt controls:

- `foreground blade passes close across the lens`
- `over-shoulder angle compresses the distance between attacker and target`
- `background opponent reacts one beat late`
- `camera holds close enough to feel danger but keeps the contact point readable`

When to avoid:

- Do not use this for full choreography clarity. It is for impact, threat, and pressure.

## Jackie-Style Environmental Action

Use when the character should feel clever, playful, scrappy, or physically inventive.

Visual grammar:

- Start from a disadvantage: cornered, outnumbered, weaponless, slipping, losing balance.
- Use the environment as choreography: chair, door, stair, railing, table, rope, fan, umbrella, bottle.
- Keep action and reaction in the same frame when possible.
- Make rhythm readable: setup -> hit/gag/action -> reaction -> next obstacle.
- Earn the finish: the final beat should resolve the setup, not just stop.

Prompt controls:

- `starts cornered with no weapon`
- `uses the table edge to redirect the attack`
- `action and opponent reaction stay in the same wide frame`
- `the final hit uses the same prop introduced at the start`

When to avoid:

- If the desired tone is godlike, tragic, or elegant, do not overuse environmental comedy.

## Anime Burst And Impact Frames

Use for manga/drama, anime-style action, explosive movement, transformation, high-speed dash, sword release, impact, supernatural power, or `燃`.

Visual grammar:

- Build anticipation: crouch, compressed shoulders, still air, eye close-up, weapon tilt.
- Use a burst: smear-like motion, speed lines, stretched limb/cloth/weapon trail, background streak.
- Add a held mid-air or pre-impact frame when the action needs grandeur.
- Use an impact frame: perspective break, shock ring, debris, flash, hard shadow, cloth snap.
- Return to a readable endpoint: character lands, weapon still, smoke clears, opponent reaction.

Prompt controls:

- `brief anticipation crouch before the dash`
- `one-frame smear-like motion trail connects start and endpoint`
- `background stretches into speed lines`
- `impact frame with debris ring and hard light flash`
- `end after the motion, body held still, cloak settling`

When to avoid:

- Do not ask Seedance to animate too many smear/impact effects in one short clip. Pick one burst and one endpoint.

## Camera Rotation And Perspective Power

Use for highly dynamic fights, aerial moves, supernatural scale, power reveal, dizzying pressure, or a character dominating space.

Visual grammar:

- Camera rotation only works when perspective relationships are clear.
- Low horizon makes the character feel larger and stronger.
- High horizon makes the character feel watched, trapped, or dominated.
- Rotate around a stable subject or stable action axis.
- Let background planes rotate with the camera, not as random scenery drift.
- Keep the subject readable through the rotation by locking silhouette or face direction.

Prompt controls:

- `camera rotates 90 degrees around the character's right side, keeping his silhouette centered`
- `low horizon line makes the figure tower over the frame`
- `background pillars rotate in perspective, showing depth`
- `rotation stops on a strong low-angle three-quarter pose`

When to avoid:

- Avoid 360 rotation if the shot has multiple characters, complex hand action, or unstable identity. Use a smaller 30-90 degree arc.

## Entrance, Reveal, And Hero Pose

Use for character introductions, transformation completion, villain reveal, godlike arrival, or `男主感`.

Visual grammar:

- Start with partial information: feet, hand, cloak edge, shadow, weapon, back silhouette.
- Delay the face reveal.
- Let environment react before the character fully acts: door light, dust, wind, crowd silence.
- Give the character negative space and a clean outline.
- Finish on a held frame: face/eyes, weapon, full-body silhouette, or back view.

Prompt controls:

- `start on boots entering a strip of light`
- `cloak edge moves before the face is revealed`
- `camera tilts up slowly from weapon hand to eyes`
- `crowd sound drops into silence`
- `end on full-body silhouette with strong negative space`

When to avoid:

- Do not reveal face, power, weapon, and action all at once. The coolness is in the delay.

## One-Take Coolness

Use for continuous walk-and-fight, corridor action, rooftop chase, moving through several spaces, or a confident long take.

Visual grammar:

- One camera logic only: follows behind, side-tracks, or leads backward.
- Mark spatial checkpoints: doorway, stair, table, corner, window, rooftop edge.
- Use foreground occlusion to hide transitions, not random cuts.
- Keep action beats separated by travel time.
- The endpoint must show changed geography or changed power status.

Prompt controls:

- `one continuous tracking shot, no cuts`
- `camera follows behind the right shoulder through the doorway, then side-tracks along the corridor`
- `foreground pillar briefly masks the transition`
- `three clean action beats separated by steps, not rapid montage`
- `end on rooftop edge with city depth opening behind him`

When to avoid:

- Do not use one-take structure for too many story beats in a short Seedance clip. Split into multiple prompts.

## Music-Driven Hype Cutting

Use for trailers, transformation montage, character hype edits, rhythm action, dance/fight sync, or `卡点`.

Visual grammar:

- Assign each beat a visual event: eye open, weapon flash, step impact, cut, power burst, pose hold.
- Alternate action and stillness so the rhythm is felt.
- Use repeated motifs: hand -> eye -> weapon -> full body -> impact.
- Let sound drop before the biggest hit.
- Do not keep the camera moving at the same intensity for the whole clip.

Prompt controls:

- `visual beat 1: eye opens on the first drum hit`
- `beat 2: cloak snaps as the camera cuts to a low-angle full body`
- `beat 3: sword flash creates a white frame`
- `music drops for half a beat before impact`
- `final beat holds on the pose instead of adding another action`

When to avoid:

- If there is no audio reference, describe rhythm as timing and visual accents rather than claiming exact music sync.

## Prompt Pattern Bank

### Cool Sword Draw

```text
Start on a quiet low-angle medium shot. The character stands still in clean silhouette, shoulders relaxed, eyes moving before the body. After a half-second pause, the thumb pushes the sword guard open two centimeters; cloth and hair remain delayed. Camera slowly pushes in from waist-up to close three-quarter profile. On the release beat, the blade slides out in one precise motion, catching a thin rim light. End with the blade held steady in the foreground, dust drifting behind, no exaggerated expression.
```

### Explosive Anime Dash

```text
Start with a compressed crouch, cloak hanging still, eyes locked forward. Silence holds for a beat. The character launches forward in one burst; a smear-like motion trail connects the start pose to the attack line, background streaks backward, cloak and hair stretch behind. Camera tracks the dash for a short lateral burst and stops on the impact frame, debris ring expanding from the contact point. End after the motion with the character already still, opponent reacting late in the background.
```

### Villain Entrance

```text
Start on the opponent's low over-shoulder foreground blur, facing a dark doorway. A shadow crosses the floor before the villain appears. Camera slowly tilts up from polished shoes to the hand, then to the eyes, keeping the face half in backlight. Room tone drops, coat hem moves in the doorway wind. The villain does not rush; only the eyes acknowledge the opponent. End on a relaxed three-quarter stance dominating the doorway.
```

### Elegant Immortal Turn

```text
Start in a calm side-profile medium shot with long sleeve and hair forming a clean silhouette. The wrist turns first, sleeve drawing a soft arc through the foreground; the body follows one beat later. Camera performs a slow chest-height orbit, keeping the face in three-quarter profile. Mist and fabric trail with delayed motion, pale rim light sliding across the cheekbone. End on a composed downward glance as the sleeve settles.
```

### Environmental Fight Beat

```text
Start with the character cornered beside a table, empty-handed and off-balance. Camera holds a medium-wide frame so both attacker and prop stay visible. The character ducks under the strike, hooks the table edge with one hand, and uses it to redirect the attacker's momentum. The opponent's reaction remains in the same frame. A cup spins off the table after the hit, marking the rhythm. End with the character recovering balance and the table now between both fighters.
```

## Failure Checks

Before delivering a `帅/酷/燃/炫` prompt, check:

- Is the shot type chosen: wide spectacle, close impact, environmental action, anime burst, rotation, entrance, one-take, or music-driven?
- Is there one main action, not a pile of cool actions?
- Is the camera movement motivated by the effect?
- Is the final frame a strong held image?
- Does the environment or sound react to the character?
- Are empty adjectives replaced by visible controls?
- For Seedance, can the model physically perform this in one short clip?
